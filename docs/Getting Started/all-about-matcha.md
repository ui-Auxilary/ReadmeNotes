Matcha is a finely ground powder made from specially grown and processed green tea leaves. Originating in China and perfected in Japan, matcha has become a beloved ingredient worldwide — prized for its vibrant color, unique flavor, and impressive health benefits.

## What Is Matcha?

Matcha is made from shade-grown tea leaves called **tencha**. Several weeks before harvest, the tea plants are covered to block direct sunlight. This increases chlorophyll production, giving the leaves their signature bright green color and boosting amino acid content. After harvesting, the leaves are steamed, dried, and stone-ground into a fine powder.

Unlike regular green tea — where leaves are steeped and discarded — with matcha, you consume the entire leaf, making it a more potent source of nutrients.

## Health Benefits

- **Rich in antioxidants:** Matcha is packed with catechins, particularly EGCG (epigallocatechin gallate), which are powerful antioxidants that help protect cells from damage.
- **Calm energy boost:** Matcha contains caffeine paired with L-theanine, an amino acid that promotes calm alertness without the jitters often associated with coffee.
- **Supports metabolism:** Studies suggest that the catechins in matcha may help boost metabolic rate and support fat oxidation.
- **Enhances focus:** The combination of caffeine and L-theanine has been shown to improve concentration and cognitive performance.
- **Detoxification:** The high chlorophyll content in matcha may aid the body's natural detoxification processes.

## Types of Matcha

| Grade | Description | Best For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ceremonial** | Highest quality, made from the youngest tea leaves. Smooth, naturally sweet flavor with minimal bitterness. | Drinking straight (whisked with water) |
| **Premium** | High quality with a balanced flavor profile. Slightly more robust than ceremonial. | Everyday drinking, light cooking |
| **Culinary** | Bolder, more astringent flavor. Designed to hold up in recipes. | Lattes, smoothies, baking, cooking |

## How to Prepare Matcha

### Traditional Method (Usucha — Thin Tea)

1. **Sift** 1–2 teaspoons of matcha powder into a bowl to remove clumps.
2. **Add** about 2 oz (60 ml) of hot water — ideally around 175°F (80°C). Avoid boiling water, as it can make the matcha taste bitter.
3. **Whisk** vigorously with a bamboo whisk (chasen) in a "W" or "M" motion until the tea is frothy and smooth.
4. **Enjoy** immediately.

### Matcha Latte

1. Sift 1–2 teaspoons of matcha into a cup.
2. Add a small splash of hot water and whisk until smooth.
3. Heat and froth your milk of choice (dairy, oat, almond, etc.).
4. Pour the frothed milk over the matcha concentrate.
5. Sweeten to taste if desired.

## Storage Tips

- Store matcha in an **airtight, opaque container** to protect it from light, air, and moisture.
- Keep it in a **cool, dark place** — or refrigerate after opening for maximum freshness.
- Use within **1–2 months** of opening for the best flavor and color.

## Fun Facts

- A single cup of matcha contains roughly the nutritional equivalent of **10 cups** of regular green tea.
- The Japanese tea ceremony, known as **Chanoyu**, has centered around matcha for over 800 years.
- Matcha literally translates to **"powdered tea"** in Japanese (抹茶).
